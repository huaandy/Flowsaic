The General Education Curriculum

The courses that comprise SAU’s general education curriculum also constitute the University’s State Minimum Core, established in accordance with ACT 747 of 2011, for implementation the fall semester 2012. ACT 747 requires colleges and universities to identify “a minimum core of courses which shall apply toward the general education core curriculum for baccalaureate degrees at state supported institutions of higher education and which shall be fully transferable between state institutions.”

From the 2026–27 Undergraduate Catalog:

All degree-seeking students shall complete a minimum of 35 semester hours of general education courses. The number of hours to be completed in each academic area is listed below. Some course options may be restricted by a student’s major requirements.

English (6 hours)

Fulfilled by completing the following two courses:

  • ENGL 1113 Composition I
  • ENGL 1123 Composition II

Fine Arts/Humanities (9 hours)

World Literature/Philosophy choice (3 hours), one of:

  • ENGL 2213 World Literature I
  • ENGL 2223 World Literature II
  • PHIL 2403 Introduction to Philosophy and Ethics

Fine Arts/Humanities choice (6 hours), two of the following (no more than one course in Art and one in Music):

  • ART 1103 Introduction to Game Development
  • ART 2013 Art Appreciation
  • ENGL 2213 World Literature I
  • ENGL 2223 World Literature II
  • HUM 2003 Film Appreciation
  • HUM 2103 Social Media in Society
  • MUS 2003 History of Rock Music
  • MUS 2013 Music Appreciation
  • MUS 2023 Music and Culture of Africa and its Diaspora
  • PHIL 2403 Introduction to Philosophy and Ethics
  • THEA 2003 Theatre Appreciation
  • THEA 2633 Fundamentals of Acting
  • 3 hours of Foreign Language: CHIN 1003 or 1013 Mandarin Chinese I or II, FREN 1053 or 1063 Elementary French I or II, SPAN 1053 or 1063 Elementary Spanish I or II

Mathematics (3–5 hours)

Fulfilled by completing one of the following courses:

  • MATH 1053 Mathematical Literacy (see note 3)
  • MATH 1023 College Algebra
  • MATH 1045 Pre-Calculus Mathematics
  • MATH 1525 Calculus I
  • MATH 2124 Applied Calculus

Science (8 hours)

Biological Science choice (4 hours), one lecture and lab combination (see note 5):

  • BIOL 1043 Introduction to Biology and BIOL 1041 Lab
  • BIOL 1203 Principles of Biology I and BIOL 1201 Lab

Physical Science choice (4 hours), one lecture and lab combination:

  • CHEM 1013 College Chemistry I and CHEM 1011 Lab
  • CHEM 1023 University Chemistry I and CHEM 1021 Lab
  • CHEM 1133 Chemistry in Society and CHEM 1131 Lab
  • GEOL 1003 Physical Geology and GEOL 1001 Lab
  • PHSC 2023 Physical Sciences and PHSC 2021 Lab
  • PHYS 1133 Physics in Society and PHYS 1131 Lab
  • PHYS 2003 College Physics I and PHYS 2001 Lab
  • PHYS 2133 Astronomy and PHYS 2131 Lab
  • PHYS 2203 University Physics I and PHYS 2201 Lab

Social Science (9 hours)

World History choice (3 hours), one of (see note 1):

  • HIST 1003 World History I
  • HIST 1013 World History II

U.S. History/Government choice (3 hours), one of (see note 2):

  • HIST 2013 United States History I
  • HIST 2023 United States History II
  • PSCI 2003 American Government: National

Social Science choice (3 hours), one of:

  • CRJU 2003 Introduction to Criminal Justice
  • ECON 2103 Principles of Microeconomics
  • FIN 2003 Personal Finance
  • GEOG 2003 Introduction to Geography
  • PSYC 2003 General Psychology
  • PSCI 2003 American Government: National
  • SOC 1003 Introduction to Cultural Anthropology
  • SOC 1013 Introduction to Africana Studies
  • SOC 2003 Introduction to Sociology

Notes

  1. All degree candidates are required to complete one three-hour course in world history by taking HIST 1003 World History I, HIST 1013 World History II, or equivalent.
  2. All degree candidates are required to complete one three-hour course in United States history or government by taking HIST 2013 United States History I, HIST 2023 United States History II, PSCI 2003 American Government: National, or equivalent.
  3. MATH 1053 Mathematical Literacy can only fulfill the mathematics general education requirement if it is a terminal math requirement for a degree. It cannot fulfill the mathematics general education requirement for any major that requires MATH 1023 College Algebra.
  4. Students majoring in Engineering, Engineering Science, or Mechanical Engineering may substitute three hours of fine arts/humanities and three hours of social science courses with higher math and/or additional science courses to fulfill these general education requirements.
  5. Students majoring in Engineering, Engineering Science, or Mechanical Engineering may substitute an alternate science course to fulfill the biological science general education requirement.

For the full requirements, including university, residency and degree-specific requirements, see Degree Requirements and Graduation in the catalog.
